Slide 2 / 17 · Problem & Motivation

The Cyber Threat Landscape in High-Speed Networks

🚨
The Operational Failure
  • Gigabit Telemetry Overload: Modern networks process >100,000 flows/sec; manual inspection is physically impossible.
  • Rule-Based Blind Spots: Traditional firewalls (Snort, Suricata) fail against zero-day and low-and-slow reconnaissance.
  • Catastrophic Alert Fatigue: High False Positive Rates (FPR) cause SOC analysts to ignore critical breach alerts.
The NetShield Approach
  • Sub-Millisecond Inference: Classify 1,000 flows in 0.394 ms (~2.5 million packets/second capability).
  • Minimal False Alarm Rate: XGBoost champion model achieves an audited 2.10% FPR on unseen test flows.
  • Defensive SOC Platform: Translates raw predictions into calibrated threat tiers and deep-dive packet inspection.
Core Metric Prioritization: Detection Rate (Recall) ≥ 86% Precision ≥ 98.8% False Positive Rate ≤ 2.1% Line Latency < 1 ms
Slide 3 / 17 · Literature Survey

Systematic Gaps in Published NIDS Literature

🔍
GAP 1: The "Accuracy Trap"

Many papers cite 99% accuracy on imbalanced data. A naive dummy model predicting "Normal" achieves 95% accuracy while letting 100% of network attacks penetrate the enterprise perimeter.

GAP 2: Rampant Data Leakage

Transformers (StandardScaler, OneHotEncoder) are frequently fit on the whole dataset before splitting, leaking statistical bounds of future test data into training and inflating test scores.

GAP 3: Impractical Deep Neural Networks

Deep CNNs/BiLSTMs introduce >100ms inference latency, require expensive GPU hardware, and collapse under minor adversarial evasion attacks (PGD/FGSM).

GAP 4: No Operational Interface

Academic papers terminate at static confusion matrices in Jupyter notebooks. No operational dashboard is provided for human security analysts to inspect live packet streams.

Slide 6 / 17 · Benchmark Data

UNSW-NB15 Benchmark & The 42-Feature Space

📊
1. Basic Flow Characteristics (13 Features)

dur, proto, service, state, spkts, dpkts, sbytes, dbytes, rate, sttl, dttl, sload, dload

Captures directional packet and byte volumes, connection duration, and Time-To-Live hop dynamics.
2. TCP Dynamics & Timing (7 Features)

sloss, dloss, sinpkt, dinpkt, sjit, djit, swin, stcpb, dtcpb, dwin

Measures inter-packet jitter, TCP window size advertisements, and sequence packet drops.
3. Content & Payload Metrics (8 Features)

tcprtt, synack, ackdat, smean, dmean, trans_depth, response_body_len, ct_srv_src

Evaluates TCP 3-way handshake round-trip latency, HTTP depth, and average payload sizes.
4. Connection Windowing & State (14 Features)

ct_state_ttl, ct_dst_ltm, ct_src_dport_ltm, ct_dst_sport_ltm, ct_dst_src_ltm...

100-connection rolling temporal counters that expose stealthy reconnaissance, port scans, and SYN floods.
Slide 7 / 17 · Pipeline Integrity

Strict Anti-Leakage Protocol & Data Preprocessing

🛡️
1. Target & ID Exclusion

id (primary key), attack_cat (multiclass label), and label (target) are completely dropped from inputs. Incoming payloads supplying them trigger HTTP 422 errors.

2. Fit Strictly on Train

StandardScaler and OneHotEncoder are fit exclusively on the 175,341 training flows. The 82,332 test flows are only passed through transform().

3. Zero-Day Handling

Categorical encoders use handle_unknown='ignore'. Unseen protocols, services, or connection states at runtime are encoded as all-zero vectors without crashing.

Pydantic Validation Rule:
class NetworkFlowInput(BaseModel): # Enforces exactly 42 raw telemetry fields
# Any attempt to pass 'label', 'attack_cat', or 'id' raises ValidationError

Slide 9 / 17 · Champion Rationale

Why XGBoost Outperforms in Production NIDS

⚡
1. Line-Rate Latency: 0.394 ms / 1k flows

While Random Forest achieves a similar F1 score, XGBoost is 9x faster (0.394 ms vs 3.56 ms). This throughput margin prevents packet drops when traffic spikes.

2. Lowest False Positive Rate (2.10%)

In enterprise SOC environments, false alarms cost thousands of analyst hours. XGBoost minimizes false alarms to 2.10%, producing the highest precision (98.87%).

3. Calibrated Probabilities

Provides continuous risk probabilities from 0.0 to 1.0, enabling the SOC to categorize threat alerts into Critical (≥90%), High (≥75%), and Medium (≥50%).

4. CPU-Friendly Deployment

Unlike deep neural networks (CNNs, BiLSTMs) that require dedicated server GPUs, XGBoost runs efficiently on standard multi-core CPUs inside lightweight Docker containers.

Slide 15 / 17 · Viva Defense

University Evaluator Viva Defense Guide

🎓
Q1: "Why UNSW-NB15 over KDD99 or NSL-KDD?"

KDD99 is 27 years old with obsolete attack patterns. UNSW-NB15 was generated on an IXIA PerfectStorm testbed, contains 9 modern attack families, and includes an official partition reflecting realistic class drift.

Q2: "Why is Accuracy an insufficient metric for NIDS?"

Attacks represent <5% of real traffic. A dummy model predicting 'Normal' 100% of the time gets 95% accuracy while missing every intrusion. Recall and False Positive Rate (2.1%) are our critical metrics.

Q3: "How did you prevent data leakage?"

We dropped the 'id' artifact and 'attack_cat' label; transformers were fit strictly on the training partition only; and incoming API requests containing metadata keys are rejected.

Q4: "Why XGBoost over Deep Neural Networks (CNN/LSTM)?"

Tabular flows do not benefit from spatial layers. Deep models introduce >100ms latency and high GPU costs. XGBoost delivers 98.6% ROC-AUC in 0.39ms on CPUs.
